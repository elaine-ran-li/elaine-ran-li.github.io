Working Papers

  1. Ran Li, Yang Liu, Zhiya Zuo, and Weiquan Wang. "Emergent Fashionable Digital Technologies and Legitimacy for Digital Ventures in Nascent Markets: Evidence from the Web3 Industry." Major Revision at MIS Quarterly (UTD24, Latest manuscript available upon request)
    • Under 2nd Round Review
    • Selected for presentation at the PolyU Jointed University Summer Workshop on Information Systems 2023 (JUSWIS 2023)
    • Selected for presentation at the HKU Jointed University Summer Workshop on Information Systems 2024 (JUSWIS 2024)

    Abstract: Digital ventures are major drivers in today's economy, yet they face high failure rates in nascent markets where they often struggle to gain legitimacy in the eyes of investors. To address this, many digital ventures embrace emergent fashionable digital technologies (EFDTs) to create a yet-to-come future to attract investors. EFDTs are digital technologies that are under development but collectively believed to have the potential to come into fashion. While existing literature has attested to the legitimacy benefits of adopting already fashionable technologies, the legitimation effects of engaging with EFDTs remain understudied. Drawing on the lens of prospective legitimation, we theorize the legitimacy effects of two forms of EFDT engagement: symbolic engagement and substantive engagement. We employed a multi-method design, comprising a panel data analysis of 276 Web3 ventures (2019 – 2022) and two online experimental studies. Our findings show that substantive engagement with EFDTs is a robust driver of legitimacy, whereas symbolic engagement increases legitimacy only when the venture is young or operates in highly nascent markets.
